# Hook Library — 50+ Proven Hook Formulas

## What Makes a Hook Work

A hook has one job: stop the scroll and create an open loop the brain needs to close.

The best hooks work because of one of these psychological triggers:
- **Curiosity gap** — they know something is coming but not what
- **Pattern interrupt** — something unexpected breaks autopilot scrolling
- **Relatability** — "that's literally me" response
- **Threat or loss** — fear of missing out or being wrong
- **Social proof** — if this many people care, maybe I should too
- **Contrarian tension** — challenges something they believed was true

## Hook Categories

### Category 1 — Shocking Fact / Statistic
Best for: News, case studies, marketing breakdowns, business topics
Psychological trigger: Curiosity gap + social proof

Formulas:
1. "[Brand] just did X. The internet lost its mind."
2. "[Number] [unit] in [timeframe]. No celebrity. No ad spend. Just [unexpected thing]."
3. "This sold out in [timeframe]. Here's why that matters for every marketer."
4. "[Number]% of [audience] now [behaviour]. Brands have no idea what to do with this."
5. "[Brand] spent $0 on ads for this campaign. They got [result] anyway."

When to use: When you have a genuinely surprising number or outcome from research. Lead with the most shocking stat you found — not the most obvious one.

Why it works: Numbers are processed differently than words. They feel concrete, credible, and provoke instant "wait, how?" responses.

---

### Category 2 — Bold Claim
Best for: Opinion pieces, hot takes, contrarian angles
Psychological trigger: Contrarian tension + curiosity gap

Formulas:
6. "This is the most genius marketing move of [year]. And nobody's talking about it."
7. "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what's actually happening."
8. "Every brand will copy this in 6 months. Most will get it completely wrong."
9. "This changed how I think about marketing forever."
10. "[Brand] just rewrote the rules. The old playbook is dead."
11. "This isn't a collab. This is a case study."
12. "Nobody in marketing is saying this out loud. So I will."

When to use: When you have a genuine strong opinion or contrarian angle. The claim must be defensible — you're making a promise the rest of the script must keep.

Why it works: Bold claims create cognitive dissonance — the viewer wants to either agree or disagree, and either way they keep watching to find out if you're right.

---

### Category 3 — Provocative Question
Best for: Educational content, mindset shifts, audience who thinks deeply
Psychological trigger: Curiosity gap + self-relevance

Formulas:
13. "What if I told you [counterintuitive truth]?"
14. "Why did [X people] stand in line for [unexpected thing]?"
15. "What does [brand] know about Gen Z that your brand doesn't?"
16. "How did [brand] get [result] without spending a dollar on ads?"
17. "What happens when anime becomes more powerful than celebrities?"
18. "Why are people paying [price] for a [cheap item]?"
19. "What would you do if your brand sold out in 3 minutes?"

When to use: When you want the viewer to answer the question in their head before you answer it for them. Creates instant engagement and investment.

Why it works: The brain cannot ignore an open question directed at it. It automatically starts searching for an answer — keeping the viewer locked in.

---

### Category 4 — Story Open
Best for: Personal experience, case studies with narrative, emotional topics
Psychological trigger: Relatability + narrative tension

Formulas:
20. "Imagine standing in a line that wraps around an entire building. For chicken."
21. "In [timeframe], [person/brand] went from [state A] to [state B]. Here's what happened."
22. "Something happened on [date] that most marketers completely missed."
23. "[Brand] made one decision. It changed everything."
24. "Picture this. [Vivid scene]. That's what [brand] created with zero ad spend."
25. "Nobody saw this coming. Not even [brand]."

When to use: When you have a genuinely dramatic story arc — a before, a turning point, and an after. The scene you paint must be visual and specific.

Why it works: Humans are wired for narrative. A scene activates more brain regions than facts alone — the viewer is transported and emotionally invested before the insight lands.

---

### Category 5 — Relatability / Shared Pain
Best for: Creator-focused content, audience who faces a specific struggle
Psychological trigger: Relatability + tribal belonging

Formulas:
26. "Nobody told me this when I started [activity]."
27. "I spent [time/money] doing [thing] the wrong way. Then I found this."
28. "Every [role] makes this mistake. I did for [timeframe]."
29. "If you've ever wondered why [common frustration], this is for you."
30. "Most [audience type] are doing [thing] backwards. Here's the right way."

When to use: When your audience shares a specific pain point or struggle. Must feel authentic — forced relatability is instantly detected and rejected.

Why it works: "That's me" recognition triggers an emotional bond before a single piece of value is delivered. The viewer trusts you before you've earned it yet.

---

### Category 6 — Exclusivity / Insider Knowledge
Best for: Trend spotting, early analysis, behind-the-scenes breakdowns
Psychological trigger: Curiosity gap + FOMO

Formulas:
31. "This hasn't hit mainstream yet. But it's about to."
32. "I've been watching this happen for [timeframe]. Now I need to talk about it."
33. "Most people scrolled past this. Here's what they missed."
34. "Before everyone starts copying this — here's why it actually worked."
35. "This is what [brand] doesn't want you to understand about their strategy."

When to use: When you have genuine early insight or analysis before the topic goes mainstream. Loses power if the topic is already saturated — use bold claim instead.

Why it works: Exclusivity triggers status-seeking behaviour. "I know something others don't" is one of the most powerful social motivators.

---

### Category 7 — Contrast / Before-After
Best for: Transformation stories, strategy pivots, case studies
Psychological trigger: Curiosity gap + loss aversion

Formulas:
36. "[Old way] is dead. [New way] just proved it."
37. "Two brands. Same audience. Completely different results. Here's why."
38. "Last year: [state A]. This year: [state B]. One decision made the difference."
39. "Everyone was doing [thing]. [Brand] did the opposite. Then this happened."
40. "The brands that understand this will win. The ones that don't — won't."

When to use: When you have a clear contrast to highlight — old vs new, right vs wrong, brand A vs brand B. The contrast must be genuine and specific.

Why it works: The brain processes contrast faster than standalone information. Opposing states create immediate tension that demands resolution.

---

### Category 8 — Pattern Interrupt (Visual/Verbal)
Best for: Saturated topics, audiences who've seen every hook format
Psychological trigger: Pattern interrupt + novelty

Formulas:
41. "[Expected thing]? No. [Unexpected reframe]. Let me explain."
42. "Stop. Before you scroll past this — [specific reason to stay]."
43. "This is not a [expected content type]. This is a [reframe]."
44. "I know what you're thinking. You're wrong. Here's what's actually happening."
45. "[Pause. Look directly at camera.] [Single unexpected sentence.]"

When to use: Sparingly. Pattern interrupts lose power with repetition. Best deployed when a topic feels overdone and you need to stand out from similar content.

Why it works: The brain is constantly filtering for novelty. Anything that breaks an established pattern triggers an involuntary attention spike.

## Hook Analysis Framework

When analysing a hook from a reference reel, answer these questions:

1. **What is the hook type?** (which category above)
2. **What psychological trigger is it activating?**
3. **What open loop does it create?** (what question does it plant in the viewer's mind?)
4. **How specific is it?** (vague hooks underperform — specificity is credibility)
5. **How long is it?** (under 10 words is ideal, under 15 is acceptable)
6. **Does it make a promise?** (and does the rest of the script keep it?)
7. **Would it work without sound?** (caption hook matters too)

## Hook Rules

- Never start with "In this video" or "Today I want to talk about"
- Never start with your name or a greeting
- The hook must work as a standalone sentence — if it needs context, it's too weak
- Specificity beats vagueness every time ("3 minutes" beats "very fast")
- The hook is a promise — the script must deliver on it
- Test your hook by reading it aloud — if you'd keep scrolling, rewrite it
