# Instagram Reels — Platform Structure & Rules

## Platform Reality

Instagram Reels is not YouTube. It's not TikTok. It has its own rules, rhythms, and viewer behaviours that every script must be built around.

Key facts:
- Average viewer decision to stay or leave: 1.7 seconds
- Primary drop-off points: 8 sec, 17 sec, 25 sec
- Optimal completion-rate length: 30-45 seconds
- Caption is read BEFORE audio on 60%+ of views (silent scrolling)
- The first frame is a static thumbnail in the feed — it must work as an image

---

## The Three Reel Lengths — When to Use Each

### 15-30 seconds — The Sniper
Use when: Single insight, one shocking fact, one strong opinion
Structure: Hook → Single point → Payoff → CTA
Danger: Too short to build credibility — needs an exceptional hook
Best for: Trends, hot takes, single statistics

### 30-45 seconds — The Sweet Spot
Use when: Case study with 2-3 points, story with a clear lesson
Structure: Hook → Setup → Re-hook → 2-3 points → Payoff → CTA
Danger: Pacing must be tight — every word must earn its place
Best for: Marketing breakdowns, brand analysis, educational storytelling

### 60-90 seconds — The Deep Dive
Use when: Complex topic needing context, multi-part breakdown
Structure: Hook → Context → Re-hook → 3-4 points → Payoff → CTA
Danger: Retention drops sharply after 45 sec — needs multiple re-hooks
Best for: Step-by-step guides, detailed case studies, opinion essays

## Instagram-Specific Script Rules

### The Silent Viewer Rule
At least 60% of viewers watch with sound off initially. This means:
- The opening visual must communicate something without audio
- Text overlays on screen should mirror (not just repeat) key phrases
- The hook must work as a caption even without hearing it spoken

Always include a caption hook suggestion with every script — the first line of the caption should be as strong as the spoken hook.

### The Thumbnail Frame
The first frame is what appears in the feed before someone taps play.
- Must have a strong visual: creator's face with a clear expression, bold text, or striking image
- No black screens, no mid-sentence text, no blurry starts
- Mark [THUMBNAIL FRAME] in the script to indicate the ideal first visual

### The Caption Strategy
Caption structure for marketing/educational content:
```
Line 1: Hook (mirrors or extends the spoken hook)
Line 2-4: 2-3 bullet points teasing the content
Line 5: CTA (different from the spoken CTA — gives a second chance)
...more if needed
#hashtags (3-5 targeted, not spammy)
```

### The Loop Play Factor
Instagram rewards reels that people replay. Design the payoff line to make replaying feel worthwhile:
- Payoff should recontextualise the hook — so rewatching the hook feels different
- End on the exact same energy you opened with — creates a satisfying loop
- Never end mid-thought or with a trailing "so yeah..."

## Platform-Specific Content Rules for Marketing/Business Reels

### What Performs in This Niche

High performers:
- Specific brand case studies with named brands (not generic "a company")
- Surprising statistics with a counterintuitive lesson
- "Here's what brand X did and what every brand can learn from it" format
- Contrarian takes on accepted marketing wisdom
- Real-time analysis of breaking news or viral moments

Low performers:
- Generic tips ("post consistently", "know your audience")
- Vague claims without evidence
- Long intros before the insight
- Asking for follows before delivering value
- Listicles without a unifying insight

### The Authority Signals
For a marketing educator, these elements build credibility:
- Specific numbers (not "a lot" — "54%", "3 minutes", "hundreds of dollars")
- Named brands (not "a fast food chain" — "Popeyes")
- Your own framing/vocabulary (not repeating what others said — your analysis)
- Confidence in delivery — no hedging language ("I think maybe possibly")
- The insight goes one level deeper than the obvious take

### Trending Formats in Marketing/Business Reels (2026)

1. **The Case Study Format**: "Brand X did Y. Here's the marketing lesson."
2. **The Myth Bust**: "Everyone says [X]. Here's why that's actually wrong."
3. **The Trend Analysis**: "This just happened. Here's what it means for your brand."
4. **The Framework Drop**: "I use this 3-step framework for [thing]. Here it is."
5. **The Comparison**: "Brand A did [X]. Brand B did [Y]. Here's why one won."

## Pacing Guide by Length

### 30-45 Second Reel Timing Map

```
0-4 sec    → HOOK
4-12 sec   → SETUP (story, facts, scene)
12-17 sec  → RE-HOOK (pivot or escalation)
17-35 sec  → BREAKDOWN (2-3 key insights)
35-42 sec  → PAYOFF (the quotable line)
42-45 sec  → CTA (clean and simple)
```

### Word Count Guide
- 30 sec reel: ~75-90 words spoken
- 45 sec reel: ~110-135 words spoken
- 60 sec reel: ~150-180 words spoken
- 90 sec reel: ~220-270 words spoken

Speak at a natural conversational pace — not rushed, not slow. These counts account for pauses and emphasis.
