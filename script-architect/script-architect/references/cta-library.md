# CTA Library — 20+ CTAs by Goal and Feel

## CTA Philosophy

The CTA is the most misunderstood part of a reel script.

Most creators treat it as a transaction: "I gave you value, now give me a follow."
The viewer feels this. And it kills the moment.

The best CTAs feel like a natural extension of the content — not a separate ask bolted on at the end. They emerge from the script's logic. They make the viewer feel like taking action is in their own interest, not the creator's.

**The rule:** If your CTA could be copy-pasted onto any other reel, it's too generic. The best CTAs are specific to the content just delivered.

## CTA Categories

### Category 1 — Save CTAs
Goal: Drive saves (highest algorithm signal on Instagram)
Tone: Utility-focused — "this is reference material"

1. "Save this. You'll want to come back to it."
2. "Screenshot the payoff line. It'll make sense again later."
3. "Save this for your next campaign meeting."
4. "Bookmark this — because every brand is about to try copy this."
5. "Save it. Share it with whoever runs marketing at your company."

**When to use:** When the content is genuinely reference-worthy — frameworks, case studies, data-backed insights. Don't use for opinion pieces or hot takes.

**What to avoid:** "Save this if you found it helpful" — weak, generic, feels like begging.

---

### Category 2 — Follow CTAs
Goal: Drive follows
Tone: Value-forward — tell them exactly what they're getting

6. "Follow for more marketing breakdowns like this every week."
7. "If you want to understand why brands win or lose — follow."
8. "I break down one brand's marketing strategy every week. Follow to stay ahead."
9. "Follow — because the brands doing this next are already making moves."
10. "New marketing case study every week. Follow so you don't miss the next one."

**When to use:** When the content clearly demonstrates the value of following — they just experienced what following delivers.

**What to avoid:** "Follow for more content" — this tells them nothing. Follow for MORE WHAT.

---

### Category 3 — Share CTAs
Goal: Drive shares and DMs
Tone: Social — "send this to someone"

11. "Send this to someone who runs a brand."
12. "Tag a marketer who needs to see this."
13. "Forward this to your team. Seriously."
14. "If you know a brand doing this wrong — send it to them."
15. "Share this with whoever manages your brand's social."

**When to use:** When the content has a clear intended audience beyond the current viewer — a business owner, a marketer, a brand manager. The more specific the target, the better.

**What to avoid:** Generic "share if you agree" — low effort, low conversion.

---

### Category 4 — Combination CTAs
Goal: Drive saves + follows together
Tone: Clean, not greedy — maximum two asks, never three

16. "Save this. Follow for the next one."
17. "Follow for more breakdowns like this — and save this one for reference."
18. "Bookmark it. Follow — because next week's case study is even better."

**When to use:** When the content genuinely earns both asks. Don't combine if the reel didn't fully deliver on its promise.

**What to avoid:** Save + follow + share + comment — greed kills credibility.

---

### Category 5 — Curiosity CTAs
Goal: Drive comments and DMs
Tone: Invites response — opens a conversation

19. "Drop a brand in the comments — I'll break down their strategy next."
20. "What brand do you want me to analyse next? Comment below."
21. "Have you seen another brand do this? Tell me in the comments."
22. "Which part of this surprised you most? Let me know."

**When to use:** When you want engagement signals or genuinely want audience input for future content. Also good for growing comment section as social proof.

**What to avoid:** "What do you think?" — too vague, too low-effort.

---

### Category 6 — Provocative CTAs
Goal: Drives shares through intellectual tension
Tone: Creates urgency without begging

23. "Most brands will try copy this next month. Most will get it wrong. Follow — I'll show you why."
24. "The brands that understand this will win the next decade. Save this for when you're ready."
25. "This is happening faster than most marketers realise. Follow to stay ahead of it."

**When to use:** When you've established genuine authority in the reel and the topic has real stakes. Must feel earned — if the reel didn't deliver, this sounds arrogant.

## CTA Rules

### The Earned CTA Rule
The CTA must feel like it comes from the content — not despite it. Ask yourself: "If someone watched this reel and did nothing, what would they regret?" That's your CTA.

### The One-Ask Rule
One primary ask per CTA. Save + follow is acceptable. Save + follow + comment + share is greedy and converts poorly.

### The Specificity Rule
Generic CTAs convert poorly. Specific CTAs convert well.
- Weak: "Follow for more content"
- Strong: "Follow for a new marketing breakdown every week"

### The Natural Delivery Rule
The CTA should sound like the creator is still talking — not like they switched into "ask mode." 
- Unnatural: "So if you enjoyed this video please like subscribe and save..."
- Natural: "Save this. Follow for the next one."

### The Length Rule
CTAs should be under 15 words. Under 10 is better.
The reel's job is done by the payoff line. The CTA is a signpost — not another paragraph.

## CTA Selection Guide

| Creator goal | Reel type | Best CTA category |
|---|---|---|
| Growing followers | Educational case study | Follow (category 2) |
| Algorithm boost | Framework or reference content | Save (category 1) |
| Viral reach | Hot take or controversial opinion | Share (category 3) |
| Community building | Audience question or poll | Curiosity (category 5) |
| Authority building | Deep analysis | Provocative (category 6) |
| Balanced growth | Strong case study | Combination (category 4) |

---

## CTA Analysis Framework

When analysing a CTA from a reference reel, answer:

1. **What category is it?** (save / follow / share / combination / curiosity / provocative)
2. **Does it feel earned?** (does the reel's content justify this ask?)
3. **How specific is it?** (does it tell viewers exactly what they're getting?)
4. **How long is it?** (under 15 words?)
5. **Does it sound natural?** (would the creator actually say this?)
6. **What would you change?** (and why would your version perform better?)
