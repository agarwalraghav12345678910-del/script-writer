# Retention — Re-hooks, Open Loops & Pacing

## Why Retention is the Real Game

Getting someone to stop scrolling is the hook's job.
Keeping them watching is retention's job.

Instagram's algorithm doesn't just reward views — it rewards watch time and completion rate. A reel watched to the end signals high quality content. A reel dropped at 8 seconds signals low quality — even if the hook was great.

The goal: make leaving feel like a loss at every moment.

---

## The Attention Curve

Attention on Instagram Reels follows a predictable pattern:

```
100% ─── Hook (0-4 sec) — spike of attention
 85% ─── First 8 seconds — still deciding to stay
 65% ─── 15-17 seconds — first major drop-off point
 50% ─── 25 seconds — second drop-off
 40% ─── 35 seconds — committed viewers only
 35% ─── End — completion rate
```

Every script must have a retention mechanism placed just BEFORE each drop-off point — not at it, before it.

---

## Re-hook Techniques

A re-hook is a moment that reignites curiosity and resets the viewer's decision to keep watching.

### Technique 1 — The Pivot Question
Place at: 12-17 seconds
Formula: "So [recap of what you just said] — but here's what nobody's asking."
Or: "That's the story. Here's the part that actually matters."
Or: "So how did this happen?"

Why it works: Signals a gear-shift is coming. The viewer who was about to leave now waits for the pivot.

---

### Technique 2 — The Escalation
Place at: 15-20 seconds
Formula: "But it gets [better/worse/stranger]."
Or: "And then this happened."
Or: "Here's where it gets interesting."

Why it works: Creates a narrative ladder — each rung promises the next is worth reaching. The viewer climbs because leaving feels like stopping before the punchline.

---

### Technique 3 — The Reframe
Place at: 12-18 seconds
Formula: "This isn't about [what they think it's about]. It's about [deeper truth]."
Or: "On the surface this looks like [X]. But what's really happening is [Y]."

Why it works: Shifts the frame of reference entirely. The viewer who thought they knew where this was going is suddenly unsure — and curiosity keeps them watching.

---

### Technique 4 — The Callback Setup
Place at: 20-25 seconds
Formula: Reference something from the hook and promise to close the loop later.
"Remember that line I opened with? Here's why I said it."
Or: "That number I mentioned? Here's what's behind it."

Why it works: The brain hates open loops. If you explicitly reference an earlier moment and promise to close it, the viewer stays to get resolution.

---

### Technique 5 — The Pace Change
Place at: Any drop-off point
Formula: Deliberately slow down or speed up delivery.
Slow: "And then — [pause] — everything changed."
Fast: Rapid-fire three facts back to back with no pause.

Why it works: Monotone delivery kills retention. Pace changes signal something new is happening — reactivating attention automatically.

---

### Technique 6 — Direct Address
Place at: 15-20 seconds
Formula: "If you're a [role/type of person] — this part is specifically for you."
Or: "Pay attention to this next part."
Or: "This is the bit most people miss."

Why it works: Being spoken to directly triggers an involuntary attention spike. Calling out a specific audience type creates belonging and urgency simultaneously.

## Open Loop Science

An open loop is an unresolved question or tension planted early in the script that only gets resolved at the end.

The best reels have one primary open loop (planted in the hook) and one or two secondary loops planted in the body.

### Primary Loop — Hook Level
Planted in: First 4 seconds
Example: "Merch sold out in 3 minutes. No celebrity. No ad spend. Just anime." 
→ Opens the loop: How? Why? What does this mean?
Closed at: The payoff line at the end.

### Secondary Loops — Body Level
Planted in: 10-20 seconds
Example: "And the brand that figured this out? They didn't even plan for what happened next."
→ Opens the loop: What happened?
Closed at: 5-10 seconds after planting.

### Loop Rules
- Never open more than 2 loops simultaneously — cognitive overload kills retention
- Always close every loop you open — broken promises destroy trust
- The primary loop close should be the payoff line — make it worth the wait
- Tease the close before you deliver it: "Here's the part I find most interesting about this..."

## Pacing Rules for Instagram Reels

### Sentence Length
- Hook and re-hook: 4-8 words per sentence
- Body/breakdown: 8-14 words per sentence
- Payoff: 6-10 words — short enough to screenshot
- CTA: Under 15 words total

### Information Density
- Don't explain everything — leave something for the comments
- One idea per sentence in the hook and re-hook
- Maximum 3 key points in the body — more than 3 loses viewers
- Each point should be 1-2 sentences maximum

### Rhythm Patterns That Retain
1. **Triple rhythm**: Three short sentences of similar length back to back creates momentum
   - "Lines around buildings. Merch gone in minutes. eBay listings for hundreds."
2. **Short-long-short**: Punchy sentence, longer explanation, punchy callback
3. **Question-answer**: Rhetorical question immediately followed by an answer creates satisfaction
4. **Contrast pair**: Two opposing statements of similar length create tension

### Delivery Notes for Scripts
Mark these in every script:
- [PAUSE] — 0.5-1 second silence for effect
- [SLOW] — deliberate pace change down
- [FAST] — speed up for energy or data dump
- [LEAN IN] — physical movement signals intimacy and importance
- [CUT] — suggest a visual edit point

## Retention Checklist (Run Before Finalising Every Script)

- [ ] Is there a re-hook placed BEFORE the 17-second mark?
- [ ] Does the hook open a loop that gets closed at the payoff?
- [ ] Are sentences in the hook and re-hook under 10 words?
- [ ] Is there a pace change somewhere in the body?
- [ ] Does the payoff line feel like it closes something — not just ends the script?
- [ ] Is there a maximum of 3 main points in the body?
- [ ] Does the CTA feel like a natural next step — not an interruption?
